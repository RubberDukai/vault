---
title: Preserving with salt, sugar and vinegar
summary: Jam, pickles and salted meat — the three ways to hold a harvest without a freezer.
serves: —
time: an afternoon
---

The freezer is the first thing to go. These three do not need it, and the handbook's preserving chapter explains why they work.

## Jam (any fruit)

Equal weights of fruit and sugar. Low-pectin fruit (strawberries, blackberries) needs a sour apple or a squeeze of lemon added. Cook the fruit soft with a little water, add the sugar, stir until dissolved, then boil hard until a spoonful on a cold plate wrinkles when pushed (about 105 °C). Pour hot into hot, clean jars; seal. Keeps a year or more in a cool dark place. If the sugar is short, halve it and keep the jam in the coolest place you have — it will last months rather than years.

## Quick pickle (any vegetable)

Bring to the boil equal parts vinegar and water with a tablespoon of salt and one of sugar per 500 ml. Pack cut vegetables (cucumber, onion, carrot, beetroot, cabbage, beans) into clean jars, pour the hot brine over to cover, seal. Ready in a day, keeps months in a cool place, longer if the vinegar is 5% strength or more and you use it neat.

## Sauerkraut (cabbage and salt only)

Shred cabbage, weigh it, add 2% of its weight in salt (20 g per kilo), massage until it weeps, pack tight into a jar so the liquid covers it, weigh it down. Leave at room temperature 1–4 weeks, tasting. Bubbles and a clean sour smell are right; fuzzy mould on top is not — skim it and carry on if what is under the brine is fine. Keeps months in the cold. The same method works with any firm vegetable.

## Salted meat and fish

Cut into pieces no thicker than 5 cm. Rub every surface heavily with coarse salt, pack in a crock or bucket with salt between the layers and a thick layer on top, weigh down. Liquid draws out within a day; keep the meat under it. Two to three weeks for meat, less for fish. Rinse and soak overnight before cooking. Keeps months in the cold; watch for any smell that is not simply salty. Smoke it afterwards over a low, cool fire for a day or two to keep it longer still.
