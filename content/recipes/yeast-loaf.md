---
title: Everyday yeast loaf
summary: The basic loaf, and how to keep the yeast going without buying more.
serves: 1 large loaf
time: 3–4 hours, mostly waiting
---

## You need

- 500 g bread flour (plain flour works, with a slightly closer crumb)
- 7 g dried yeast, or 15 g fresh, or 100 g of active sourdough starter
- 10 g salt (2 teaspoons)
- 320 ml warm water
- A spoon of oil or butter if you have it

## Method

1. Mix flour, yeast and salt (keep the salt off the yeast until they are stirred in). Add the water and bring to a rough dough.
2. Knead 10 minutes by hand until smooth and stretchy — it should spring back when poked. Or: no kneading, just fold it over on itself four times every half hour for two hours.
3. Cover and leave somewhere warm until doubled: one to two hours.
4. Knock back, shape into a loaf, put in a greased tin or on a tray. Cover and leave to rise again until nearly doubled, 45–60 minutes.
5. Bake at 220 °C for 30–35 minutes. Hollow when knocked underneath.

## Keeping yeast alive without a shop

Save a piece of dough the size of an egg from each batch. Keep it in a jar with a little flour and water, feed it a spoon of flour and water daily in warm weather, every few days in cold. This is a sourdough starter — it will rise the next loaf (use 100 g of it, give it a longer rise, four to six hours). Wild yeast from the air and the flour will keep it going indefinitely. A starter that smells sharply of alcohol is hungry; one with pink or orange growth is dead — throw it out and begin again with flour and water left out for three days.
