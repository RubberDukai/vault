---
title: A stew of what there is
summary: The method that turns any vegetables, any grain and any scrap of meat into dinner for a household.
serves: 6
time: 1–2 hours
---

Not a recipe so much as a shape. Learn the shape and you can feed people from a garden and a store cupboard for a year.

## The shape

1. **Fat and onion.** A spoon of fat or oil in a big pot. Onions, leeks or the white of spring onions, cooked slowly until soft and sweet. Ten minutes. This is the base of the flavour; do not rush it.
2. **The hard vegetables.** Carrot, swede, turnip, parsnip, celery, potato — cut small, stirred in, a few minutes.
3. **The protein.** Whatever there is: a little meat cut small, a handful of lentils or soaked beans, a tin of anything. A small amount of meat flavours a whole pot; it does not need to be the bulk.
4. **Liquid.** Water, stock, the water from cooking vegetables, a splash of anything sour (cider, vinegar, tomato). Enough to cover by two fingers. Salt, pepper, a bay leaf or any dried herb.
5. **Time.** Lid on, the gentlest simmer, an hour or more. A hay box does this without fuel.
6. **The soft things last.** Cabbage, greens, peas, beans from the garden, fresh herbs — in for the last ten minutes so they keep some life.
7. **Thicken** if you like: a spoon of flour stirred into a little cold water, added and simmered five minutes. Or crush some of the potato into it.

Serve on grains, with bread, or alone. It is better the next day. It freezes, it bottles, it stretches with more water and more grain when there are more mouths.
