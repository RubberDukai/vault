# Spanish — how it works

Spanish is the fastest major language for an English speaker to become useful in. The spelling is almost perfectly phonetic, half the vocabulary is recognisable, and the grammar — though it has more moving parts than English — is regular enough that a few patterns carry you a long way.

## The sounds

Five vowels, always the same, never reduced to a mumble:

| | Sounds like |
|---|---|
| **a** | f**a**ther |
| **e** | b**e**t |
| **i** | mach**i**ne |
| **o** | m**o**re (short) |
| **u** | fl**u**te |

Consonants to watch:

- **j**, and **g** before e or i — a throaty *h*, like clearing the throat gently. *Jamón, gente.*
- **ll** and **y** — like English *y* in most of the world, sometimes *j* or *sh* in Argentina. *Calle, llamar.*
- **ñ** — *ny* as in ca**ny**on. *Año, mañana.*
- **rr**, and **r** at the start of a word — rolled. *Perro, río.* A single **r** between vowels is a light tap. *Pero* (but) and *perro* (dog) differ only by the roll.
- **h** — always silent. *Hola* is *ola*.
- **z**, and **c** before e or i — *th* in Spain, *s* in the Americas. Either is understood everywhere.
- **v** and **b** sound the same, a soft *b*.
- **qu** — *k*. *Que* is *ke*.

**Stress** falls on the second-to-last syllable if the word ends in a vowel, *n* or *s*; on the last syllable otherwise. Any exception is marked with an accent: *está, médico, canción.* Get stress wrong and a word can change meaning: *hablo* (I speak), *habló* (he spoke).

## Nouns have a gender

Every noun is masculine or feminine, and the words around it agree. Mostly you can tell: nouns ending in **-o** are usually masculine, **-a** feminine. *El libro, la casa.* Exceptions are learned with the word — *el día, la mano, el problema, el agua* (feminine, but *el* because *la agua* is awkward to say).

| | Masculine | Feminine |
|---|---|---|
| the | **el** libro · **los** libros | **la** casa · **las** casas |
| a / some | **un** libro · **unos** libros | **una** casa · **unas** casas |

Plurals: add **-s** to a vowel, **-es** to a consonant. *Casa, casas. Ciudad, ciudades.*

Adjectives agree too, and usually **follow** the noun: *la casa blanca, los libros nuevos.*

## Two verbs for "to be"

The famous one. Both mean "to be"; they are not interchangeable.

**ser** — what something *is*: identity, origin, profession, character, time. Permanent-ish.
*Soy médico. Es de Londres. Son las tres.* I am a doctor. He is from London. It is three o'clock.

**estar** — where something *is* and how it is *right now*: location, condition, mood, ongoing action.
*Estoy en casa. Está enfermo. Estamos cansados.* I am at home. He is ill. We are tired.

The classic pair: *Es aburrido* — he is boring. *Está aburrido* — he is bored.

| | ser | estar |
|---|---|---|
| I | soy | estoy |
| you | eres | estás |
| he / she / you (formal) | es | está |
| we | somos | estamos |
| they / you (plural) | son | están |

## Verbs — the three families

Every verb ends in **-ar**, **-er** or **-ir**, and each family conjugates by a pattern. The **present tense** of the regular ones:

| | habl**ar** (speak) | com**er** (eat) | viv**ir** (live) |
|---|---|---|---|
| yo (I) | habl**o** | com**o** | viv**o** |
| tú (you) | habl**as** | com**es** | viv**es** |
| él / ella / usted | habl**a** | com**e** | viv**e** |
| nosotros (we) | habl**amos** | com**emos** | viv**imos** |
| ellos / ustedes | habl**an** | com**en** | viv**en** |

Because the ending tells you who, **the subject pronoun is usually dropped**: *Hablo español* — I speak Spanish. Add *yo* only for emphasis.

**The present covers the near future too**: *Mañana voy al mercado.* Tomorrow I go to the market.

**Irregulars you cannot avoid**, present tense, first person then the pattern:

- **tener** (have): tengo, tienes, tiene, tenemos, tienen. Also "to be" for age, hunger, thirst, cold, fear: *tengo hambre, tengo frío, tengo 30 años.*
- **ir** (go): voy, vas, va, vamos, van. **Ir a + verb** = going to: *Voy a comer.* I'm going to eat. This is the everyday future.
- **hacer** (do, make): hago, haces, hace, hacemos, hacen. Also weather: *hace frío*, it's cold.
- **poder** (can): puedo, puedes, puede, podemos, pueden. *¿Puede ayudarme?* Can you help me?
- **querer** (want): quiero, quieres, quiere, queremos, quieren. *Quiero agua.*
- **saber** (know a fact) sé, sabes… **conocer** (know a person or place) conozco, conoces…
- **decir** (say): digo, dices, dice, decimos, dicen.
- **venir** (come): vengo, vienes, viene, venimos, vienen.

**The past**, in two flavours: the **preterite** for completed events (*comí* — I ate, *fui* — I went, *hizo* — he did) and the **imperfect** for background and habits (*comía* — I used to eat, I was eating). Learn the preterite of the irregulars above first; it is what stories and reports are made of.

## Negation and questions

**Negation:** *no* before the verb. *No hablo español. No tengo agua.* Double negatives are correct: *No tengo nada* — I have nothing.

**Questions:** raise the voice, or flip the order. Written with ¿ at the start. *¿Tienes agua? ¿Dónde está el hospital?*

| | |
|---|---|
| ¿Qué? | What? |
| ¿Quién? | Who? |
| ¿Dónde? | Where? |
| ¿Cuándo? | When? |
| ¿Cómo? | How? |
| ¿Por qué? | Why? |
| ¿Cuánto? | How much? |
| ¿Cuál? | Which? |

## Tú and usted

**Tú** is you, informal — family, friends, children, anyone your age in a casual setting. **Usted** is you, formal — strangers, elders, officials — and takes the third-person verb form (*usted tiene*, *usted es*). When in doubt, use *usted*; nobody was ever offended by too much respect. Most of Latin America uses **ustedes** for all plural "you"; Spain has an informal plural, *vosotros*, which you can ignore.

## Object pronouns

Come **before** the verb: *Lo tengo* — I have it. *Me llamo Ana* — I call myself Ana (my name is Ana). *¿Me entiende?* — Do you understand me? *Te ayudo* — I'll help you.

## Learning it

1. The vowels and the *r*. Say every word out loud from day one.
2. The survival phrases until they are automatic.
3. **Ser, estar, tener, ir, querer, poder** — six verbs that carry half of conversation.
4. The regular present tense. Then *ir a* + verb for the future.
5. Vocabulary in the hundreds, from the decks and from whatever you can read.
6. The preterite, and you can tell what happened yesterday.
