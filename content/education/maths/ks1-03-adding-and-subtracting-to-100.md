# Adding and subtracting to 100

**What they need first:** bonds to 10 and 20 automatic; counting to 100 in ones and in tens; reading two-digit numbers and knowing which digit is the tens (*Number and place value*).

## Teaching it

**Tens and ones, with things.** Bundle sticks into tens with string (or use beans in cups of ten). 34 is three bundles and four loose. Make numbers, say them, write them. "Which digit tells us how many bundles?" This is place value, and everything below depends on seeing it.

**Adding tens.** 34 + 20: two more bundles, the loose ones do not change — 54. Do lots: 23 + 10, 45 + 30, 61 + 20, on the sticks, then in the head, then on the hundred square (a 10 × 10 grid of 1 to 100 — draw one and pin it up; adding ten is moving down a row).

**Adding ones without crossing a ten.** 34 + 5: five more loose ones — 39. Easy; the bonds do it.

**Adding ones across a ten.** 34 + 8. Three ways, in order:
1. On the hundred square: count on 8, and watch the row change — 42.
2. Bridging: 34 needs 6 to get to 40; 8 is 6 and 2; so 40 + 2 = 42. (This is the bonds-to-10 lesson doing its work.)
3. On the sticks: 4 loose and 8 loose is 12 loose — that is a bundle and 2 — so 4 bundles and 2: 42. *Ten ones became one ten.* Say it every time. This is "carrying", and understood this way it never needs to be a rule.

**Adding two two-digit numbers.** 34 + 27: tens with tens (30 + 20 = 50), ones with ones (4 + 7 = 11), then 50 + 11 = 61. Do it on sticks first, then written like this:
```
  34 + 27
  30 + 20 = 50
   4 +  7 = 11
  50 + 11 = 61
```
The column method (see *Arithmetic by hand*) is the same thing written tighter; it can wait until this is easy.

**Subtracting tens and ones** the same way: 56 − 20 (take two bundles), 56 − 4 (take four loose), and then the hard one, 52 − 7, where there are not enough loose ones: *unbundle a ten* — one bundle becomes ten loose, so 4 bundles and 12 loose; take 7 — 4 bundles and 5: 45. Or bridge: 52 − 2 gets to 50, then take 5 more: 45. Or count up: from 7 to 52 is 3 (to 10) + 42 = 45.

**Checking.** Every subtraction can be checked by adding back: 52 − 7 = 45, so 45 + 7 should be 52. Make the check a habit now.

**Money.** Pence and pounds are tens and ones with a name: 10p coins are bundles, 1p coins are loose. Play shop.

## Every morning, five minutes

Bonds to 20, doubles, and now: "add ten", "take ten", "add nine" (add ten, take one).

## Exercises

1. Write in bundles and loose ones: 47, 70, 19, 83.
2. 45 + 10,  45 + 30,  72 − 20,  90 − 40.
3. 23 + 6,  51 + 7,  38 − 5,  67 − 4.
4. 27 + 6,  48 + 5,  35 + 9,  56 + 8.
5. 32 − 5,  41 − 7,  60 − 3,  74 − 8.
6. 25 + 34,  46 + 38,  57 − 23,  82 − 46.
7. A hen lays 26 eggs in one week and 19 the next. How many altogether?
8. There are 100 sheep. 37 are taken to the far field. How many stay?
9. Check each of your answers to question 6 by adding or subtracting back.

## Answers

1. 4 bundles 7 loose; 7 bundles 0 loose; 1 bundle 9 loose; 8 bundles 3 loose.
2. 55, 75, 52, 50.
3. 29, 58, 33, 63.
4. 33, 53, 44, 64.
5. 27, 34, 57, 66.
6. 59, 84, 34, 36.
7. 45 eggs.
8. 63 sheep.
9. 59 − 34 = 25 ✓; 84 − 38 = 46 ✓; 34 + 23 = 57 ✓; 36 + 46 = 82 ✓.

## Going further

Three numbers at once (23 + 15 + 12); adding to 1,000 with bundles of a hundred (ten bundles in a box); and the written column method in *Arithmetic by hand*, which this lesson has made obvious.
