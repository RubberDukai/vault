# Negative numbers and the start of algebra

## Negative numbers

**What they are.** Numbers below zero. Temperature is the natural example: −5 °C is five degrees below freezing. A debt, a depth below sea level, a step backward — anything with a direction and a zero.

**The number line** is the tool. Draw it, with zero in the middle, positives to the right, negatives to the left. Every rule below is just movement along it.

- **Adding** moves right. **Subtracting** moves left. 3 − 5: start at 3, move 5 left, land on −2.
- **Adding a negative** is subtracting: 3 + (−5) = 3 − 5 = −2.
- **Subtracting a negative** is adding: 3 − (−5) = 3 + 5 = 8. Taking away a debt makes you richer.
- **Multiplying:** same signs give positive, different signs give negative. (−3) × (−4) = 12. (−3) × 4 = −12. Same for dividing.

Children get the multiplication rule by memorising it. Give them the reason, once: (−3) × 4 is "−3, four times" = −12, obviously. Then (−3) × (−4) must be the *opposite* of that, because multiplying by −4 is the opposite of multiplying by 4. So 12.

**Exercises:** −7 + 3 · 4 − 9 · −2 − 6 · −2 − (−6) · (−5) × 3 · (−5) × (−3) · 12 ÷ (−4) · The temperature was −4 °C at dawn and rose 11 degrees by noon. What was it at noon? · A diver is at −15 m and descends a further 8 m. Where is she?

## Algebra — a letter for a number you don't know

That is all algebra is at the start. Nothing more mysterious.

*"I'm thinking of a number. I double it and add 3. I get 11. What was it?"*

Write the number as *n*. Then: 2*n* + 3 = 11. The question is now a puzzle you can solve by **undoing** each step in reverse order: take away 3 (2*n* = 8), then halve (*n* = 4).

**The one rule:** whatever you do to one side of the equals sign, do to the other. The equation is a balance. Add 3 to the left, add 3 to the right. Halve the left, halve the right. It stays true.

## Why bother

Because the letter lets you write a rule once that works for every number.

The area of any rectangle: *A* = *l* × *w*. The cost of *n* loaves at 40p: *C* = 40*n*. How long a tank lasts: days = litres ÷ litres used per day. Each is a formula, and a formula is algebra.

Practical formulas from this handbook that children can be given as algebra:

- Rain collected: *litres* = *roof area* × *mm of rain* × 0.85
- Solar panel needed: *watts* = *daily Wh* ÷ *sun hours* ÷ 0.7
- Voltage drop in a cable: *V* = 2 × *L* × *I* × 0.0172 ÷ *mm²*
- Oral rehydration: *sugar* = 6 teaspoons per *litre*

Make them **rearrange** one. If *V* = *I* × *R*, then *I* = *V* ÷ *R* and *R* = *V* ÷ *I*. Rearranging is just undoing.

## Simplifying

Like terms combine. 3*n* + 2*n* = 5*n*, the same way three apples and two apples are five apples. But 3*n* + 2 stays as it is — you cannot add apples to a number.

Brackets multiply out: 3(*n* + 2) = 3*n* + 6. Everything inside gets multiplied.

## Exercises

1. Solve: *n* + 7 = 12 · 3*n* = 21 · *n* − 4 = −1 · 2*n* + 5 = 17 · *n*/3 = 4 · 5*n* − 3 = 2*n* + 9
2. Simplify: 4*a* + 3*a* · 7*b* − 2*b* + *b* · 2(*x* + 5) · 3(*y* − 2) + 4
3. Write a formula for the number of legs in a field with *c* chickens and *g* goats.
4. A tank holds *T* litres and the household uses *d* litres a day. Write a formula for how many days it lasts. If *T* = 1,000 and *d* = 45, how many days?
5. Rearrange *distance* = *speed* × *time* to find *time*. If you walk at 5 km/h, how long does 12 km take?
6. "I think of a number, subtract 4, and triple the result. I get 18." Write it as an equation and solve it.
7. The temperature drops 3 degrees every hour from 6 °C. Write a formula for the temperature after *h* hours. When does it reach −9 °C?

## Answers

1. n = 5; n = 7; n = 3; n = 6; n = 12; n = 4.
2. 7a; 6b; 2x + 10; 3y − 2.
3. legs = 2c + 4g.
4. days = T ÷ d; 1,000 ÷ 45 = 22 days (just over).
5. time = distance ÷ speed; 12 ÷ 5 = 2.4 hours, that is 2 hours 24 minutes.
6. 3(n − 4) = 18, so n − 4 = 6, so n = 10.
7. temperature = 6 − 3h; −9 = 6 − 3h gives 3h = 15, h = 5 hours.

## Checking they have it

Ask them to **make up** a "think of a number" puzzle for you, write it as an equation, and check your answer. If they can build one, they understand it.
