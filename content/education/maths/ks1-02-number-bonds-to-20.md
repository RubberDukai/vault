# Number bonds, and adding and subtracting to 20

**What they need first:** counting to 20 forwards and backwards, reading and writing the numerals, and knowing that a number is a quantity (*Number and place value*).

## Why bonds matter

A child who knows that 7 + 3 = 10 *without counting* can do 47 + 3, 70 + 30, 7 + 4 (it is 10 and one more), 13 − 7 (take 3 to get to 10, then 4 more) — everything. A child who counts on fingers for 7 + 3 will count on fingers at fourteen. The whole aim of this lesson is to make the bonds automatic. It takes a few weeks of five minutes a day, and it is the best-spent five minutes in the whole curriculum.

## Teaching it

**Bonds to 10.** Take ten beans (stones, buttons, dried peas). Put them in two hands. "How many in this hand? How many in that? Ten altogether." Do it again with a different split. Write each split down as they find it: 0 + 10, 1 + 9, 2 + 8, 3 + 7, 4 + 6, 5 + 5, and then the same the other way round. Notice together that there are only six pairs. Make a picture of them — a staircase of beans. Play it as a game: you say a number, they shout the partner that makes 10. Every day until it is instant.

**Bonds to 20** the same way, once 10 is solid: 11 + 9, 12 + 8, 13 + 7, 14 + 6, 15 + 5 — and point out that they are the bonds to 10 with a ten added.

**Adding within 20 — three ways, in this order:**
1. *Counting all* (5 + 3: count out 5 beans, count out 3, count them all — 8). This is where they start.
2. *Counting on* (5 + 3: say "five" and count on three more — six, seven, eight). Put the bigger number first: 3 + 5 becomes 5 + 3.
3. *Bridging through 10* (8 + 5: 8 needs 2 to make 10, so take 2 from the 5, leaving 3; 10 + 3 = 13). This is the goal, and it only works when the bonds are automatic.

**Subtracting within 20** — the same three ways: taking away and counting what is left; counting back; and bridging (13 − 5: take 3 to get to 10, then take 2 more: 8). And the fourth way, the important one: **subtraction is the difference between two numbers**, so 13 − 5 is also "what do I add to 5 to get 13?" — count up from 5: 5 to 10 is 5, 10 to 13 is 3, so 8.

**The number line.** Draw 0 to 20 on a strip of paper and pin it up. Jumps forward are adding, jumps back are subtracting. Every child should be able to picture it with eyes shut.

**Writing it down.** Introduce the signs + − = as words first: "and", "take away", "makes". Write the sum only after it has been done with objects. Insist on **=** meaning "is the same as", not "the answer is": show that 3 + 4 = 7 and 7 = 3 + 4 and 3 + 4 = 5 + 2 are all true.

**Doubles and near-doubles.** 6 + 6 = 12 is worth knowing on its own; then 6 + 7 is "double 6 and one more". Learn the doubles to 10 + 10 as a chant.

## Every morning, five minutes

Ten quick questions, spoken, answered at once: bonds to 10, bonds to 20, doubles. Keep a tally of the score. When it is 10 out of 10 for a week, move on to the next lesson and keep the warm-up.

## Exercises

1. Write all the pairs that make 10.
2. Fill the gap: 6 + __ = 10;  __ + 3 = 10;  10 = 8 + __;  15 + __ = 20;  __ + 12 = 20.
3. Add: 7 + 5,  9 + 6,  8 + 8,  6 + 7,  4 + 9,  3 + 8 + 2.
4. Take away: 12 − 4,  15 − 8,  20 − 7,  11 − 9,  18 − 9,  16 − 8.
5. What number is missing? 8 + __ = 13;  __ − 5 = 9;  14 − __ = 6.
6. There are 20 eggs. 12 are used for cooking. How many are left?
7. Tom has 7 conkers and finds 8 more. How many now? He gives 6 away. How many then?
8. True or false? 5 + 4 = 4 + 5;  9 − 3 = 3 − 9;  6 + 6 = 7 + 5;  10 = 10 − 0.

## Answers

1. 0+10, 1+9, 2+8, 3+7, 4+6, 5+5 (and the reverses).
2. 4; 7; 2; 5; 8.
3. 12, 15, 16, 13, 13, 13.
4. 8, 7, 13, 2, 9, 8.
5. 5; 14; 8.
6. 8 eggs.
7. 15 conkers; then 9.
8. True; false (9 − 3 = 6, but 3 − 9 is not 6); true; true.

## Going further

Bonds to 100 in tens (30 + 70), then in any numbers (37 + 63) — the same idea one size up, and the doorway to the next lesson. A pack of cards with the picture cards removed makes a bonds game: turn two, do they make 10?
