# Times tables 2, 5 and 10, and sharing

**What they need first:** counting in twos, fives and tens (stepping along the number line); adding to 100.

## Teaching it

**Multiplication is "groups of".** Lay out 3 groups of 2 beans. "How many groups? How many in each? How many altogether?" 3 groups of 2 is 6. Write it as 3 × 2 = 6, and read × as "groups of" or "lots of" for now. Lay out 2 groups of 3: also 6. *The order does not matter* — show it with a rectangle of beans, 3 rows of 2 or 2 rows of 3, turned round. This is the most useful fact in the tables: it halves what has to be learned.

**Repeated addition.** 4 × 5 is 5 + 5 + 5 + 5. Count in fives along the number line: 5, 10, 15, 20. Every table is just counting in steps.

**The 10s** first: 1 × 10 = 10, 2 × 10 = 20 … the answer is the number with a 0 after it. Easy, and it makes the 5s easy: **5 × anything is half of 10 × it** (5 × 6 = half of 60 = 30). The pattern in the 5s: answers end in 5 or 0, alternately.

**The 2s** are the doubles they already know from the bonds lesson: 2 × 7 = double 7 = 14. Answers are always even — 0, 2, 4, 6, 8 at the end.

**Learn them three ways.** Chant them (2, 4, 6, 8… and "two twos are four, three twos are six…"); write them out as a list and look at the patterns; and quick-fire them out of order until each one is instant. Out of order is what counts: "seven fives?" — "thirty-five" with no chanting up from the start.

**Division as sharing.** 12 beans shared between 3 people, one each round and round: 4 each. 12 ÷ 3 = 4. Read ÷ as "shared between".

**Division as grouping.** 12 beans, how many groups of 3 can I make? 4 groups. 12 ÷ 3 = 4 again — the same sum, a different question. Both meanings matter: "24 eggs shared among 4 people" is sharing; "24 eggs, how many boxes of 6?" is grouping.

**Multiplying and dividing undo each other.** 3 × 4 = 12, so 12 ÷ 4 = 3 and 12 ÷ 3 = 4. Every table fact is three facts. Show it with the rectangle of beans: 3 rows of 4, or 12 shared into 3 rows.

**Remainders.** 13 shared between 3: 4 each and one left over. Write 13 ÷ 3 = 4 remainder 1. Real life is full of remainders.

## Every morning, five minutes

Ten table questions out of order, mixed with "what is 20 shared between 5?" and "how many 2s in 14?". Add the tables to the bonds warm-up; keep both.

## Exercises

1. Draw 4 groups of 5 dots. Write the multiplication. Draw it as a rectangle turned the other way. Write that multiplication.
2. 3 × 2,  6 × 2,  9 × 2,  4 × 5,  7 × 5,  8 × 10,  10 × 10,  5 × 10.
3. Count in 5s from 0 to 50. Count in 2s from 20 down to 0.
4. 10 ÷ 2,  18 ÷ 2,  20 ÷ 5,  35 ÷ 5,  40 ÷ 10,  70 ÷ 10.
5. Three facts from one: if 6 × 5 = 30, write the two divisions.
6. 15 apples shared between 5 children. How many each? 15 apples in bags of 5. How many bags?
7. Each hen lays 2 eggs a week. How many eggs from 8 hens? How many hens for 20 eggs?
8. 17 ÷ 5. How many, and how many left over?
9. Which of these numbers are in the 2 times table? 14, 25, 30, 41, 18. Which are in the 5 times table? Which in both?

## Answers

1. 4 × 5 = 20; 5 × 4 = 20.
2. 6, 12, 18, 20, 35, 80, 100, 50.
3. 0, 5, 10, 15, 20, 25, 30, 35, 40, 45, 50; 20, 18, 16, 14, 12, 10, 8, 6, 4, 2, 0.
4. 5, 9, 4, 7, 4, 7.
5. 30 ÷ 5 = 6; 30 ÷ 6 = 5.
6. 3 each; 3 bags.
7. 16 eggs; 10 hens.
8. 3 remainder 2.
9. 2s: 14, 30, 18. 5s: 25, 30. Both: 30.

## Going further

The 3s and 4s next (4s are double the 2s), then the rest through KS2 (*Arithmetic by hand*). A tables grid, 1 to 10 each way, drawn by the child and pinned up, shows all the patterns at once — the diagonal of square numbers, the symmetry.
