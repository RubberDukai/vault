# Shapes, measuring, time and money

**What they need first:** counting to 100, and the tables lesson helps for time and money but is not essential.

## Shapes

**Flat (2-D) shapes.** Cut them from card: circle, triangle, square, rectangle, pentagon, hexagon, octagon. Count the **sides** and the **corners** (vertices) of each; write a table. Go on a shape hunt through the house and the garden — windows, wheels, roof gables, honeycomb. Sort a pile of shapes by number of sides; then by "has a curved side / has none". A square is a special rectangle; say so.

**Solid (3-D) shapes.** Cube (dice), cuboid (a box), sphere (a ball), cylinder (a tin), cone, pyramid. Count **faces, edges and vertices** on the real objects. Which roll? Which stack? Why is a tin a cylinder and not a cube? (It rolls; it has no corners to dent.)

**Symmetry.** Fold a paper shape in half so the halves match: that fold is a line of symmetry. Find the lines on a square (four), a rectangle (two), a circle (endless), a letter A (one), a letter F (none). Paint one side of a fold and press.

**Position and turning.** Left, right, above, below, in front, behind; quarter turn, half turn, three-quarter turn, whole turn; clockwise and anticlockwise. Give and follow instructions across the room.

## Measuring

The idea, in every case: **choose a unit, count how many.** Start with body units (hand-spans, feet, paces) and discover the problem — Dad's hand-span and the child's give different answers for the same table — and so agree a fixed unit.

- **Length:** centimetres and metres. A ruler, a tape, a metre stick (make one). Measure ten things; write a table; put them in order. "About how long is …?" — estimate first, always.
- **Weight (mass):** grams and kilograms. A balance (a coat hanger with two bags) compares; kitchen scales measure. A bag of sugar is a kilogram; a paperclip is about a gram.
- **Capacity:** millilitres and litres. A litre bottle; a measuring jug; how many cups fill the bottle?
- **Temperature:** the thermometer, hot and cold, degrees.
- **Comparing:** longer/shorter, heavier/lighter, more/less, and the symbols < and > (the crocodile eats the bigger number).

## Time

**The words first:** morning, afternoon, evening, night; yesterday, today, tomorrow; the seven days in order; the twelve months; the four seasons and what happens in each. Chant them.

**Reading the clock.** A clock with hands, made from a paper plate and a split pin. The **short hand** points to the hour; the **long hand** points to the minutes. **O'clock** — long hand straight up. **Half past** — long hand straight down. **Quarter past** — long hand at the 3. **Quarter to** — long hand at the 9, and the short hand is nearly at the *next* hour. Practise every day at real times: "what time is it now?". There are 60 minutes in an hour and 24 hours in a day; the long hand counts in fives round the clock, which is why the 5 times table matters.

**How long?** A minute — count to 60 while the long hand moves one mark. An hour — a lesson and a play. A day — a sleep to a sleep.

## Money

Coins: 1p, 2p, 5p, 10p, 20p, 50p, £1, £2. Notes: £5, £10, £20. There are 100 pence in a pound (which is bundles-of-ten again). Play shop: make prices, pay, give change. "Which coins make 27p?" — as few as possible. "I pay 50p for something costing 35p; what change?" — count up from 35 to 50. If the money changes or disappears, the arithmetic does not: whatever people use to trade needs counting and change.

## Exercises

1. Draw a triangle, a square, a rectangle, a pentagon and a hexagon. Write the number of sides and corners under each.
2. Name a solid shape that rolls and one that does not. How many faces has a cube? How many corners?
3. Draw all the lines of symmetry on a square. How many has a rectangle?
4. Measure with a ruler: your thumb, this book, a spoon, a shoe. Write the answers in centimetres, smallest first.
5. Which is heavier, 1 kg of feathers or 1 kg of stones? How many 250 ml cups fill a 1 litre jug?
6. Draw clocks showing: 3 o'clock, half past 7, quarter past 10, quarter to 6.
7. What time is one hour after half past 2? Half an hour after quarter past 9?
8. Which coins make 43p using as few as possible? What change from £1 for something costing 65p?
9. Put in order, smallest first: 2 m, 50 cm, 1 m, 120 cm.
10. Write the days of the week in order, starting with Monday. Which month comes after August? Which season is January in?

## Answers

1. Triangle 3 sides 3 corners; square 4 and 4; rectangle 4 and 4; pentagon 5 and 5; hexagon 6 and 6.
2. Rolls: sphere, cylinder, cone; does not: cube, cuboid, pyramid. A cube has 6 faces and 8 corners.
3. Four on a square; two on a rectangle.
4. Answers will vary — check the ruler is read from 0, not from its end.
5. Neither — both are 1 kg. Four cups.
6. Check: short hand on the hour (a little past it for half past and quarter past; nearly at the next for quarter to), long hand at 12, 6, 3 or 9.
7. Half past 3; quarter to 10.
8. 20p + 20p + 2p + 1p (four coins); 35p change.
9. 50 cm, 1 m, 120 cm, 2 m.
10. Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday, Thursday, Friday, Saturday, Sunday; September; winter.

## Going further

Time to five minutes, then to the minute; the 24-hour clock; perimeter (add the sides); and drawing shapes to a measured size with a ruler. In the garden: measure the beds, weigh the harvest, time the boiling of an egg.
