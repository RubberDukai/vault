# Halves, quarters and thirds

**What they need first:** sharing (division) from the tables lesson; halving and doubling.

## Teaching it

**Equal parts.** Fold a sheet of paper in half. Are the two parts the same size? That is what "half" means — **equal** parts. Fold it unequally and ask if those are halves. They are not, and that is the whole idea in one fold. Write ½: "one of two equal parts". The bottom number says how many equal parts; the top says how many of them we have.

**Quarters.** Fold in half and half again: four equal parts, each a quarter, ¼. Two quarters make a half — show it on the paper. Four quarters make the whole.

**Thirds.** Harder to fold; cut a strip into three equal lengths with a ruler, or share 12 beans into 3 equal piles. ⅓.

**Halves and quarters of shapes.** Colour half a square in three different ways (a rectangle, a triangle, an L). Colour a quarter of a circle. Draw a rectangle split into thirds.

**Fractions of a group.** Half of 8 beans: share into two equal piles — 4. A quarter of 12: share into four piles — 3. A third of 9 — 3. Fractions of amounts are *sharing*, which they already know. Half of 10 cm on the ruler is 5 cm; half an hour is 30 minutes; a quarter of a pound is 25p.

**Counting in halves and quarters.** Along a number line: 0, ½, 1, 1½, 2, 2½… and 0, ¼, ½, ¾, 1. Cut apples: two halves and a half is one and a half apples.

**Fair shares in real life.** One cake between four: quarters. Three pancakes between two children: a pancake and a half each — say it, draw it. A litre of milk shared between four cups: a quarter of a litre, 250 ml, each.

## Exercises

1. Draw a square. Colour half. Draw another and colour a quarter. Draw a circle and colour three quarters.
2. Which of these show halves? (Draw shapes cut into two equal and two unequal parts.)
3. Half of 6, half of 14, half of 20, half of 50.
4. A quarter of 8, a quarter of 20, a quarter of 100.
5. A third of 6, a third of 15, a third of 30.
6. How many quarters make a half? How many quarters make a whole? How many halves make three wholes?
7. Count on in halves from 3 to 6.
8. Six eggs are shared between three people. What fraction of the eggs does each get? How many eggs is that?
9. A ribbon is 24 cm long. Half is cut off. Then half of what is left is cut off. How long is the last piece?
10. Which is more: half of 20 or a quarter of 48?

## Answers

1. Check the coloured part is an equal share: half is 2 of 4 equal parts, a quarter 1 of 4, three quarters 3 of 4.
2. Only the equal cuts.
3. 3, 7, 10, 25.
4. 2, 5, 25.
5. 2, 5, 10.
6. Two; four; six.
7. 3, 3½, 4, 4½, 5, 5½, 6.
8. A third each; 2 eggs.
9. 6 cm.
10. A quarter of 48 is 12; half of 20 is 10; so a quarter of 48.

## Going further

Tenths (a ruler is a picture of them: 10 mm in a centimetre) lead straight to decimals in KS2; fractions of a turn (a quarter turn is 90 degrees) lead to angles. *Fractions, ratio and measuring* takes it on.
