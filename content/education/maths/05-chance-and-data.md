# Chance, data and deciding

This is the mathematics of making decisions when you do not know what will happen. Which crop to plant, whether to travel today, how much to store. It is also the mathematics of not being fooled.

## Three kinds of average

Given the days of rain per month over a year: 12, 9, 14, 8, 10, 7, 6, 8, 11, 15, 16, 13.

**Mean** — add them all, divide by how many. 129 ÷ 12 = 10.75. What people usually mean by "average".

**Median** — put them in order, take the middle one (or the mean of the middle two). 6, 7, 8, 8, 9, 10, **11, 12**, 13, 14, 15, 16 → (11 + 12) ÷ 2 = 11.5.

**Mode** — the most common value. 8.

**Why three?** Because the mean is dragged by extremes. Five households' grain stores: 100 kg, 120 kg, 90 kg, 110 kg, 2,000 kg. Mean 484 kg — "the average household has nearly half a tonne". Median 110 kg — the honest picture. One rich household distorts the mean; the median ignores it. **When someone quotes an average, ask which one.**

**Range** — biggest minus smallest — says how spread out things are. Two villages both average 10 days of rain, but one ranges 8–12 and the other 0–25. The second is a far riskier place to farm.

## Probability

The chance of something, as a number from 0 (impossible) to 1 (certain). Written as a fraction, decimal or percentage.

- A fair coin: heads ½, or 0.5, or 50%.
- A fair six-sided die: any particular number ⅙. An even number 3/6 = ½.
- **Probability = ways it can happen ÷ total ways.**

**Two things both happening:** multiply. Two coins both heads: ½ × ½ = ¼. Three days in a row of rain, if each day is independently 30%: 0.3 × 0.3 × 0.3 = 0.027, about 3%.

**Either of two things happening** (when they can't both): add. Rolling a 1 or a 2: ⅙ + ⅙ = ⅓.

**Something not happening:** 1 minus the chance it does. If 20% of seeds fail, 80% germinate.

## Expected value — the number for deciding

Multiply each outcome by its probability, add them up. That is what you get *on average* if you did it many times.

**Two fields.** Field A yields 400 kg reliably. Field B yields 700 kg in a good year (60% chance) and 100 kg in a bad one (40%).

Expected yield of B: 0.6 × 700 + 0.4 × 100 = 420 + 40 = **460 kg**. Higher than A.

So plant B? **Not necessarily.** If 100 kg means the family goes hungry, a 40% chance of that is unacceptable however good the average. Expected value tells you the long-run average; it does not tell you whether you can survive the bad case. **For anything you cannot afford to lose, plan for the bad case, not the average.** That single sentence is most of risk management.

## Reading a number someone gives you

- **"Half of all…"** Half of what? Of how many? A survey of four people is not a survey.
- **"Doubled the risk."** From what to what? 1 in a million doubled is 2 in a million. 1 in 10 doubled is 1 in 5. The relative change means nothing without the base.
- **"On average."** Which average? See above.
- **"Nine out of ten."** Nine out of ten *what*, chosen *how*? If they picked the ten, the number is theirs, not the world's.
- **Coincidence is common.** Roll enough dice and you will see six sixes eventually. A run of good luck is not evidence of a method; a run of bad luck is not a curse.
- **Small samples lie.** Three good harvests from a new seed proves very little. Thirty proves something.

## Recording data so it is worth something

A notebook with a column per thing, a row per day. Weather, temperature, what was planted, what was harvested, who was ill. **Consistent, dated, honest, kept for years.** In a world with no records, this is how you learn which week to plant, which field floods, which winter is the bad one. The science lesson **How to find things out** is the other half of this.

Then draw it. A **bar chart** for comparing amounts. A **line graph** for something changing over time. A picture shows a pattern the numbers hide.

## Exercises

1. Find the mean, median, mode and range of: 3, 7, 7, 2, 11, 6, 7, 5.
2. Egg counts per day for a week: 4, 5, 3, 6, 5, 0, 5. Mean and median. Why might one day be zero, and which average is more useful for planning?
3. A bag holds 5 red beans and 3 white. Chance of picking red? Of picking white twice in a row, putting the first back?
4. If each of three water filters independently fails with a 10% chance in a year, what is the chance that all three fail? That at least one survives?
5. Crop A: 300 kg guaranteed. Crop B: 80% chance of 500 kg, 20% chance of nothing. Expected value of each. Which would you plant, and what would change your mind?
6. A neighbour says a remedy "cured three people last winter." What would you need to know before believing it works?
7. Keep a table of the temperature at dawn for two weeks. Plot it. What is the range? Is there a trend?

## Answers

1. Mean 6 (48 ÷ 8); median 6.5 (the middle two are 6 and 7); mode 7; range 9.
2. Mean 4; median 5. A zero day might be a broody or ill hen, a fox scare, or eggs missed; the median ignores the odd day and is the better guide to a normal day.
3. 5/8; 3/8 × 3/8 = 9/64.
4. 0.1 × 0.1 × 0.1 = 0.001, one in a thousand; at least one survives 0.999.
5. A: 300 kg. B: 0.8 × 500 = 400 kg expected. B has the higher expected value but a one-in-five chance of nothing; if a failed harvest means hunger, plant A (or some of each). What changes your mind: stores in hand, how bad a zero would be, whether the 80% is trustworthy.
6. How many people took it and did *not* get better; how many got better without it; whether the three would have recovered anyway; how "cured" was judged; whether the person telling you sells it.
7. Answers vary. Range is highest minus lowest; a trend is a steady rise or fall across the fortnight rather than day-to-day wobble.
