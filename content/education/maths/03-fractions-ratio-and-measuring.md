# Fractions, ratio and measuring

This is the most immediately practical mathematics there is. Every recipe, every dose, every building measurement and every fair division is this lesson.

## Fractions are division

**¾ means 3 ÷ 4.** That is not a separate fact to learn; it is what the notation means. The bottom number (denominator) says how many equal parts the whole is cut into. The top (numerator) says how many you have.

**Equivalent fractions:** multiply top and bottom by the same number and the value is unchanged, because you are multiplying by 1. ½ = 2/4 = 4/8 = 50/100.

**Adding** needs a common denominator — you cannot add thirds to quarters any more than you can add sticks to stones:
½ + ⅓ → 3/6 + 2/6 = **5/6**

**Multiplying** is straightforward: multiply across the top, multiply across the bottom.
⅔ × ¾ = 6/12 = **½**

**Dividing** — turn the second fraction upside down and multiply. ½ ÷ ¼ = ½ × 4/1 = **2**. Which makes sense: there are two quarters in a half.

## Ratio and proportion

A ratio compares quantities. **2:1 sand to cement** means for every 2 buckets of sand, 1 of cement — however many buckets you use in total.

This is the single most useful piece of maths in the whole handbook, because it appears everywhere:

| Task | Ratio |
|---|---|
| Mortar | 4 sand : 1 cement |
| Concrete | 3 gravel : 2 sand : 1 cement |
| Two-stroke fuel mix | 50:1 or 25:1 petrol to oil — **check the engine** |
| Oral rehydration | 1 litre water : 6 tsp sugar : ½ tsp salt |
| Fermenting brine | 2–3 g salt per 100 g of vegetables and water |
| Diluted urine as fertiliser | 10 water : 1 urine |

**Scaling a recipe.** A recipe for 4 people needs 600 g flour. For 6 people: 600 ÷ 4 = 150 g per person, × 6 = **900 g**. Do it per unit and it never goes wrong.

## Percentages

**Per cent means "out of a hundred".** 25% is 25/100 is ¼.

- To find 10%, divide by 10. To find 1%, divide by 100. Build everything else from those: 30% is 10% three times; 15% is 10% plus half of it.
- **Increase by 20%:** multiply by 1.2. **Decrease by 20%:** multiply by 0.8.
- Watch the trap: a 50% fall followed by a 50% rise does not return you to where you started. 100 → 50 → 75.

## Measuring

**Length:** 10 mm = 1 cm, 100 cm = 1 m, 1,000 m = 1 km.
**Mass:** 1,000 g = 1 kg, 1,000 kg = 1 tonne.
**Volume:** 1,000 ml = 1 litre. And the fact that makes everything easy: **1 litre of water weighs 1 kg**, and occupies 1,000 cm³.

Useful conversions to know by heart:

| | |
|---|---|
| 1 inch | 2.54 cm |
| 1 foot | 30.5 cm |
| 1 metre | 3.28 feet |
| 1 mile | 1.61 km |
| 1 kg | 2.2 pounds |
| 1 gallon (UK) | 4.55 litres |
| 1 gallon (US) | 3.79 litres |
| 0 °C | 32 °F |
| °C to °F | × 9/5, then + 32 |

**Body measures** for when you have no ruler — measure your own and write them down:
a hand span, a forearm (roughly 45 cm), a pace, an outstretched arm span (roughly your height), a thumb width (roughly 2 cm).

## Area and volume

- **Rectangle area** = length × width. **Triangle** = ½ × base × height. **Circle** = π × r², with π ≈ 3.14.
- **Box volume** = length × width × height. **Cylinder** = π × r² × height.

Practical uses: how much seed for a plot, how many tiles for a roof, how much water a barrel holds, how much firewood a stack contains, how much grain fits in a bin.

A cylindrical water butt 0.5 m across and 1 m tall: radius 0.25 m, so π × 0.0625 × 1 = 0.196 m³ = **196 litres**.

> **Note** Scaling catches people out. Double the side of a square and the area quadruples. Double the size of a barrel in every dimension and it holds **eight** times as much. This is why a small animal loses heat faster than a large one, and why a scale model is never a reliable guide to the real thing.

## Exercises

1. ¾ + ⅝, ⅔ − ¼, ⅗ × ⅔, ¾ ÷ ½
2. Mortar at 4:1. You have 20 buckets of sand. How much cement? What if you only have 3 buckets of cement — how much sand can you use?
3. A recipe for 6 uses 450 g of grain. Rewrite it for 4 people, and for 15.
4. Two-stroke at 25:1. How much oil for 5 litres of petrol? Give the answer in millilitres.
5. A rectangular plot is 12 m by 8 m. What is its area? If potatoes yield 3,000 kcal per m², how many calories is that, and how many days would it feed one person needing 2,200 kcal a day?
6. A water tank is 1.2 m long, 0.6 m wide and 0.8 m deep. How many litres?
7. Convert: 6 feet to cm; 20 miles to km; 100 °F to °C.
8. A wound needs irrigating with at least 500 ml per small wound. Your bottle holds 1.5 litres. How many small wounds can you treat?
