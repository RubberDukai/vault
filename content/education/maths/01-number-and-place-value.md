# Number and place value

## What the teacher needs to know

Everything in arithmetic rests on one idea: **the position of a digit tells you its value.** A child who genuinely understands this can work out arithmetic they were never taught. A child who does not will memorise procedures that collapse under pressure.

Do not rush this. It is worth weeks.

## The idea

In the number **347**:

- the **7** means seven ones
- the **4** means four tens, which is forty
- the **3** means three hundreds

So 347 is 300 + 40 + 7. The same digit means something different depending on where it sits: in **77**, the left 7 is worth ten times the right one.

We use ten as our base because we have ten fingers. Nothing deeper than that.

## Teaching it

**Start with real objects.** Stones, beans, sticks, buttons. Counting is physical before it is symbolic.

1. **Count to twenty** with objects, moving each one as it is counted. The child must understand that the last number said is *how many there are*, which is not obvious to a young child.
2. **Bundle into tens.** Ten sticks tied with string is one bundle. Now count out 34: three bundles and four loose. This single activity is place value.
3. **Write it down.** Three bundles and four loose is written 34. Ask what 43 would be — four bundles and three loose. Let them see that the order changes the amount.
4. **Bundle the bundles.** Ten bundles of ten make a hundred. Now build 347 physically.
5. **Zero as a placeholder.** Build 306. There are no bundles of ten, so we must write something in that column or the number reads as 36. That is the whole job of zero, and it took humanity a very long time to invent it.

## Exercises

1. Write these in words and say what each digit is worth: 58, 205, 940, 1,006.
2. What is the value of the 6 in each? 16, 64, 640, 6,004.
3. Make the largest number you can from the digits 4, 0, 7, 2. Now the smallest. Explain why.
4. Count out 100 dry beans. Bundle them into tens. How many bundles?
5. A number has 4 hundreds, 0 tens and 9 ones. Write it.

## Going further

- **Other bases.** Show them counting in fives on one hand. Computers count in twos: 1, 10, 11, 100, 101 means 1, 2, 3, 4, 5. Nothing about ten is special, and seeing that is genuinely illuminating.
- **Roman numerals.** MCMXLVII. Try multiplying two Roman numerals and you will see instantly why place value was such an enormous invention.
- **Big numbers.** A thousand thousands is a million. If you counted one number every second without stopping, a million takes about eleven and a half days. A billion takes nearly thirty-two years.

## Checking they have it

Ask: *"Why is 40 bigger than 14, when they use the same digits?"*

If they explain it in terms of tens and ones, they understand. If they say "because it just is", go back to the bundles.
