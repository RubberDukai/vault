# Materials — what things are made of

**What they need first:** the sorting habit from *Living things*.

## Naming and describing

Go round the house touching things and naming what each is **made of**, not what it is: the table is *wood*, the window *glass*, the tap *metal*, the cup *pottery* (clay), the jumper *wool*, the bag *plastic*, the wall *stone* or *brick*, the rope *fibre*, the shoe *leather* and *rubber*. Some things are made of several: a knife is metal and wood. Collect one small sample of each material into a box.

**Words for materials** — collect them on a wall chart with the samples: hard/soft, rough/smooth, bendy/stiff, stretchy, shiny/dull, see-through (transparent)/not, waterproof/absorbent, heavy/light, warm/cold to touch, strong/weak, floats/sinks, magnetic. Describe a material using five words; the other person guesses which.

## Testing fairly

A material's properties are found out by **testing**, and a test must be **fair**: change only one thing. Which paper is strongest? Same size strip of each, same weight hung from each, see which tears. Which cloth soaks up most water? Same size square of each, same puddle, same time. Which material keeps a hot drink hot? Same water, same start temperature, a jar wrapped in each, feel (or measure) after ten minutes. Write the results in a table: material, what happened, best to worst. Predict first, then test, then compare: the surprise is the science.

**Magnets.** Which of the samples does a magnet pick up? Only some metals — iron and steel — not copper, not aluminium, not gold. A magnet finds a dropped needle in the straw and tells you whether a tin is really tin.

**Float or sink.** Wood floats, stone sinks, metal sinks — except a metal boat. Why? (Shape; the air inside. Push a floating bowl under and feel the water push back.) Make a boat from foil and load it with beans until it sinks; count the beans; change the shape; count again.

## The right material for the job

Why is a window glass and not wood? Why is a saucepan metal and not plastic? Why is a raincoat not made of paper? Each answer is a property: see-through, does not melt, waterproof. Then the reverse: what would happen if a spoon were made of chocolate, a chair of paper, a roof of cloth? Design a tent, a bucket, a knife handle, a bandage — choose the material and say why.

**Natural or made.** Wood, stone, wool, cotton, leather, clay come from living things or the ground; glass, metal, plastic, paper, brick are *made* by people from those, usually with fire. A brick is clay that was heated. Glass is sand that was heated. This is the bridge to the next thing.

## Changing materials

- **Bending, squashing, stretching, twisting** — clay bends and stays bent; a rubber band stretches and springs back; a stick bends and breaks; a wire bends and stays. Sort by what happens.
- **Heating** — butter melts, then goes back to solid when cool (**reversible**); an egg cooks and never goes back (**not reversible**); bread toasts; ice becomes water becomes steam (see *Seasons and weather*); a candle melts *and* burns, which are two different things.
- **Cooling** — water becomes ice; melted chocolate sets; steam on a cold window becomes water again.
- **Mixing** — salt in water disappears (**dissolves**) and can be got back by drying the water off; sand in water does not; oil sits on top; flour and water make dough, which is new and cannot be unmixed.

Do all of these at the stove and the sink; it is cooking, with questions.

## Exercises

1. What are these made of? A window, a spoon, a jumper, a brick wall, a rope, a wellington boot.
2. Describe wood, glass and wool with three words each from the chart.
3. Plan a fair test to find which of three cloths dries fastest. What must be the same? What is the only thing that changes?
4. Which of these does a magnet pick up? A steel nail, a copper coin, a plastic button, a paper clip, a wooden peg.
5. Why is a saucepan made of metal and a saucepan handle often made of wood or plastic?
6. Which of these can be changed back? Melted butter; a cooked egg; ice; a burnt match; dissolved salt.
7. Name one natural material and one made material, and say what the made one is made from.
8. Load a foil boat with beans until it sinks. Write how many. Make it wider and try again. What happened?

## Answers

1. Glass; metal; wool; brick (clay); fibre (or plastic); rubber.
2. For example: wood — hard, stiff, floats; glass — hard, see-through, smooth; wool — soft, stretchy, warm.
3. Same size of cloth, same amount of water, same place, same start time; only the cloth changes.
4. The steel nail and the paper clip.
5. Metal takes heat well and does not burn or melt on the stove; wood and plastic do not carry the heat, so the handle stays cool.
6. Melted butter, ice and dissolved salt can be changed back; the egg and the match cannot.
7. For example: wool (natural); glass (made, from sand).
8. Answers vary; a wider boat usually holds more because it pushes more water aside.
