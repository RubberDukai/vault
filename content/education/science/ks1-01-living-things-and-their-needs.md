# Living things and what they need

**What they need first:** nothing but curiosity and a garden or a windowsill.

## Alive, dead, or never alive

Put objects on the table: a stone, a leaf, a snail, a stick, a coin, a seed, a feather. Sort them into three piles: **alive**, **was alive once**, **never alive**. Argue about the seed (alive, waiting) and the stick (was alive). What do all the living things do? Together, find the list: they **move, grow, feed, breathe, make more of themselves, sense the world, and get rid of waste**. A fire moves and eats and grows — is it alive? No: it does not sense, and it never came from a parent fire. This argument is the whole of biology at five.

## Plants and animals

Two great kinds of living thing. **Plants** make their own food from sunlight, water and air, and stay put. **Animals** eat plants or other animals, and move to find them. Sort pictures or real finds. Fungi (mushrooms) are neither, and children like knowing that.

**What every plant needs:** light, water, air, warmth, and something to grow in. **Prove it.** Four bean seeds in four jars on damp paper: one in the light with water; one in a dark cupboard with water; one in the light with no water; one in the fridge. Look every day; draw what you see; the one in the cupboard grows pale and tall and falls over (it is *searching* for light — the best moment in the experiment), the dry one does nothing, the cold one waits.

**The parts of a plant** and their jobs: **roots** (hold it up, drink), **stem** (carries water up, holds the leaves to the light), **leaves** (make the food from sunlight), **flower** (makes the seeds), **fruit** (carries the seeds), **seed** (the next plant, packed with a lunch). Pull up a weed and find every part. Cut open an apple and a tomato and find the seeds. Plant them.

**What every animal needs:** food, water, air, shelter, and — for many — company. The chickens, the dog, the cat, the family: what does each need each day, and who provides it? A child who feeds the hens every morning is learning this lesson better than any page.

## The body

Name the parts on the child, out loud, from the head down: head, hair, eyes, ears, nose, mouth, teeth, neck, shoulders, arms, elbows, hands, fingers, chest, tummy, back, legs, knees, feet, toes. Then the **five senses** and the part that does each: **see** (eyes), **hear** (ears), **smell** (nose), **taste** (tongue), **touch** (skin, everywhere). A blindfold game: name it by touch, by smell, by sound.

**Growing up.** Baby, toddler, child, teenager, adult, old person. Which can you do now that you could not as a baby? What will you be able to do at ten? Measure the child against the door frame and date the mark; do it every birthday.

**Keeping the body well.** It needs the same as any animal: food (of different kinds — grow a rainbow on the plate), water, air, sleep, exercise, and to be kept clean. Why we wash hands before eating: tiny living things too small to see, which the next lesson meets.

## Animals close to home

Choose one creature to watch for a season: a snail in a jar with damp earth and lettuce (let it go after a week), the birds at a feeder, the hens, a spider in the corner, the tadpoles in a bucket of pond water. Draw it; write what it eats, how it moves, what it does when you touch it. Learn its name and look it up in the encyclopedia. This is what a scientist does: watch carefully, write it down, compare.

**Sorting animals** by what they have: fur (mammals), feathers (birds), scales (fish, reptiles), a shell or no bones at all (snails, worms, insects — count the legs: six is an insect). By what they eat: plant-eaters, meat-eaters, both.

## Exercises

1. Sort into alive / was alive / never alive: a cloud, a cat, a candle, a carrot, a chair, a caterpillar.
2. List seven things all living things do.
3. Draw a plant and label root, stem, leaf, flower. Say what the root does and what the leaf does.
4. Which bean grew best and why? Which one grew tall and pale, and what was it looking for?
5. Draw yourself and label ten parts of the body.
6. Match the sense to the part: seeing, hearing, smelling, tasting, touching — eyes, skin, nose, ears, tongue.
7. What does a hen need every day? Who gives it to her?
8. Watch one animal for ten minutes. Write three things it did.
9. Six legs, feathers, fur, scales: name an animal for each.

## Answers

1. Alive: cat, carrot (if fresh), caterpillar. Was alive: chair (wood), candle (if beeswax or tallow — a good argument). Never alive: cloud.
2. Move, grow, feed, breathe, reproduce, sense, get rid of waste.
3. Root holds the plant and takes in water; leaf makes the food from light.
4. The light-and-water bean; the cupboard bean was searching for light.
5. Any ten of the parts named in the lesson.
6. Seeing – eyes; hearing – ears; smelling – nose; tasting – tongue; touching – skin.
7. Food, clean water, shelter, safety from the fox, company; the child (and it should be true).
8. Any three observations, written as what was seen rather than guessed.
9. For example: beetle, robin, fox, trout.
