# Handwriting and spelling

**What they need first:** the letter sounds from *Learning to read*, and the ability to hold a pencil and draw a line where they mean to.

## Handwriting

**Grip.** Pencil between thumb and first finger, resting on the middle finger, about 2 cm from the point; the hand rests on the paper. Fix it now, gently, every time — a bad grip is painful at twelve.

**Big before small.** Letters in the air with a whole arm; in a tray of sand or flour with a finger; on a slate or a fogged window; then on paper with lines. Say the movement aloud: "c — start at the top, round, and stop".

**The letter families** — teach them in groups that share a movement, not in alphabet order:
- **Curly caterpillars** (start at the top and go round anticlockwise): c a d g q o e s f
- **Ladder letters** (start with a straight line down): l i t u j y
- **One-armed robots** (down, back up, and over): r n m h b k p
- **Zig-zag monsters**: v w x z

Practise one family a week: a line of each letter, best three ringed, then the letters in words. Lower-case first; capitals when the lower-case is fluent, taught as "the same letter, bigger and different".

**On the line.** Tall letters (b d f h k l t) touch the top; small letters sit between; tails (g j p q y) hang below. Draw the lines yourself: 8 mm apart to start, 6 mm by seven.

**Numbers** the same way: 0 to 9, the same size as tall letters, and the ones children reverse — 3, 5, 7 — watched for.

Ten minutes a day, and it is done by seven. Joined writing comes in KS2 once the letters are automatic.

## Spelling — the order

English spelling looks lawless and is about three-quarters regular. Teach the regular part in this order; each step lets the child write hundreds more words.

1. **One letter, one sound.** Words like *cat, dog, sun, pin, hot* — three sounds, three letters. They can spell these the day they can read them: say the word slowly, listen for each sound, write its letter. ("Sound it out" works for spelling as well as reading.)
2. **Two letters, one sound.** *sh, ch, th, ng, ck, ll, ss, ff, zz* — *ship, chop, this, ring, duck, bell, kiss, off, buzz*. The doubled letters at the end of a short word is the first rule they meet.
3. **Adjacent consonants.** *st, sp, tr, fl, gr, mp, nd, nt* — *stop, spin, trap, flag, grip, lamp, hand, tent*. Four sounds, four letters; the trick is hearing both consonants.
4. **The long vowels, first spellings.** *ai, ee, igh, oa, oo* — *rain, feet, night, boat, moon*; then *ay* (at the end: *day*), *ea* (*sea*), *ie* (*pie*), *oe*/*ow* (*toe, snow*), *ue*/*ew* (*blue, new*).
5. **Magic e.** *hop → hope, cap → cape, pin → pine, cub → cube*. The *e* on the end makes the vowel say its name. Whole weeks on this; it unlocks thousands of words.
6. **The other vowel sounds.** *ar* (*car*), *or* (*for*), *er/ir/ur* (*her, bird, turn* — three spellings, one sound: learn which words take which by reading them), *ow/ou* (*cow, out*), *oi/oy* (*coin, boy*), *air/ear* (*hair, near*).
7. **Endings.** *-s* and *-es* for plurals (*cats, boxes*), *-ing*, *-ed* (which sounds like *t* in *jumped*, *d* in *played*, *id* in *wanted* — but is always written *-ed*), *-er, -est*, *-ly*, *-y*.
8. **The rules for adding endings.** Double the last consonant after a short vowel (*hop → hopping*, *run → running*); drop the *e* (*hope → hoping*); change *y* to *i* (*cry → cried*).
9. **The tricky words** — the fifty or so that break the rules and appear everywhere: *the, was, said, of, to, do, come, some, one, once, are, were, there, their, they, here, where, what, who, you, your, love, have, give, little, water, house, friend, people, again, because, could, would, should, any, many, does, goes, two, four, eight, Mr, Mrs.* Learn them by look-cover-write-check, five a week, and use them in sentences. The "look, say, cover, write, check" routine: look at the word, say the letters, cover it, write it, uncover and check letter by letter.
10. **Homophones**, once they read fluently: *to/too/two, there/their/they're, hear/here, sea/see, be/bee, blue/blew, night/knight, one/won, son/sun*.

## How to run spelling

Ten words a week from the current step, tested on Friday, the wrong ones carried into next week. Spelling is learned by **writing words**, not by chanting letters: every word spelled is then used in a sentence. Dictation once a week — you read a sentence, they write it — is the best test there is: *The ship had a red flag. My cat sat on the mat and went to sleep.*

Keep a **personal spelling book**: every word they get wrong in their own writing goes in, in alphabetical order, and it is the first place to look. It becomes their dictionary.

## Exercises

1. Write a line of each letter in the curly caterpillar family. Ring your best one of each.
2. Sound out and write: cat, ship, ring, duck, bell, stop, hand, lamp, tent.
3. Write the long-vowel words: rain, feet, night, boat, moon, day, sea, snow, blue.
4. Add magic e and say what happens: hop, cap, pin, cub, tap, kit, not.
5. Write these tricky words three times each, then use each in a sentence: the, was, said, they, because.
6. Add -ing: hop, run, hope, make, cry, jump. Add -ed: play, jump, want, hop, cry.
7. Dictation (an adult reads): *The dog ran to the shop. We had fish and chips. Can you see the moon at night?*
8. Which is right? *I have too / two cats. Put it over their / there. Can you hear / here me?*

## Answers

1. Check the start point (top, going anticlockwise) and that letters sit on the line.
2. As written; watch for *sh* not *sh-h*, and the doubled *ck, ll*.
3. As written.
4. hope, cape, pine, cube, tape, kite, note — the vowel now says its name.
5. Any sensible sentence with the word spelled correctly.
6. hopping, running, hoping, making, crying, jumping; played, jumped, wanted, hopped, cried.
7. Mark for the sounds spelled with the right letters and a capital and full stop on each sentence.
8. two; there; hear.

## Going further

Joined handwriting in KS2; the *-tion, -sion, -ous, -ible/-able* endings; prefixes *un-, dis-, re-, pre-*; and the dictionary, which is the real spelling book — and lives on the Library shelf as Wiktionary.
