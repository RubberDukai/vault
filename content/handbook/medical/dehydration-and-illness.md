# Dehydration, fever and common illness

## Oral rehydration solution

If you remember one thing from this handbook, remember this recipe. Oral rehydration therapy is credited with saving tens of millions of lives, mostly children, and it is sugar, salt and clean water.

**Per 1 litre of clean water:**

- **6 level teaspoons of sugar**
- **½ level teaspoon of salt**

Stir until dissolved. **It should taste no saltier than tears.** If it tastes saltier than that, you have made it wrong — throw it out and start again, because too much salt makes dehydration worse.

The sugar is not for energy. Glucose and sodium are absorbed together by the gut wall through a shared transporter, and the water follows them. That coupling is why this works when plain water does not, even in someone with severe diarrhoea.

Add a mashed banana or a little fruit juice for potassium if you have it. Make fresh daily.

**How to give it:** small sips, continuously. A few teaspoons every couple of minutes. A vomiting person can usually keep down small frequent sips when they would immediately bring up a glassful. Do not stop giving fluids because they are vomiting — slow down and keep going.

**How much:** roughly a quarter to half a litre after every loose stool for an adult; for a child under two, a quarter of a cup. Keep feeding them normally alongside it — the old advice to rest the gut was wrong.

## Spotting dehydration

| Severity | Signs |
|---|---|
| Mild | Thirst, dry mouth, darker urine, mild headache |
| Moderate | Little urine and dark, sunken eyes, no tears when crying, lethargy, skin slow to spring back when pinched |
| **Severe** | No urine for 8+ hours, very sunken eyes, cold mottled hands and feet, fast weak pulse, confusion or unresponsiveness |

**Severe dehydration is life-threatening** and normally needs intravenous fluids. Without them: give ORS continuously by whatever route you can, keep them cool and lying down, and do not give up — many people can be pulled back with sustained oral fluids alone.

**Children and infants dehydrate far faster than adults** because of their size-to-surface ratio. A baby with diarrhoea can go from well to dangerously dehydrated in hours. Watch for a sunken fontanelle in infants, no wet nappy for six hours, and floppy unresponsiveness. These are emergencies.

## Fever

Fever is the immune system working, not the disease itself. It does not need suppressing unless the person is genuinely uncomfortable or at risk.

- **Fluids first.** Fever increases fluid loss substantially.
- Paracetamol or ibuprofen for comfort. Never aspirin for a child or teenager with a viral illness — it risks Reye's syndrome.
- Cool cloths and light clothing. Not cold baths, which cause shivering and drive core temperature up.

**Worrying signs:** a stiff neck with headache and light sensitivity; a rash that does not fade when pressed with a glass; a fever over 38 °C in a baby under three months; confusion; difficulty breathing; a fever that returns after several days of improvement; any fever lasting more than five days.

## Diarrhoea and vomiting

Usually self-limiting, usually viral, and almost always about fluids rather than drugs.

- ORS, continuously.
- Eat when able — bland starchy food, and continue breastfeeding infants throughout.
- **Do not use anti-diarrhoeal drugs like loperamide if there is blood in the stool or a high fever.** Slowing the gut traps an invasive infection inside.
- Isolate the patient from food preparation and enforce handwashing rigorously. This is how one sick person becomes ten.

**Blood in the stool, a high fever, severe abdominal pain, or symptoms lasting more than a few days** suggests something more serious — dysentery, typhoid, cholera — and needs medical treatment if it exists.

## Respiratory illness

Most coughs and colds are viral and need rest, fluids and time. Look for the ones that are not:

- **Fast or laboured breathing**, ribs pulling in with each breath, grunting, or nostrils flaring in a child
- Blue or grey lips or fingertips
- Chest pain, coughing blood
- Inability to speak a full sentence in one breath
- Confusion or extreme drowsiness

Pneumonia in a world without antibiotics is a serious matter. Nurse them sitting upright rather than flat, keep fluids going, keep the air humid, and rest is not optional.

## Pain relief you can hold

- **Paracetamol / acetaminophen** — fever and pain. Do not exceed the stated maximum; overdose causes liver failure that is silent for the first day and then fatal. Be especially careful with combination remedies that also contain it.
- **Ibuprofen** — pain and inflammation. Take with food. Avoid with stomach ulcers, kidney problems, dehydration or asthma in some people.
- They work by different mechanisms and can be alternated for stronger effect.
- **Willow bark** contains salicin, the natural precursor of aspirin, and has genuine analgesic effect as a tea. Same cautions as aspirin: not for children with viral illness, not for people with ulcers or on blood thinners.

## Prevention, which beats all of the above

- Clean water and rigorous handwashing prevent most of what is on this page.
- **Vaccinations, up to date, for everyone in the household, now.** Tetanus, and everything else on the schedule. There is no home substitute for any of them.
- Keep teeth clean — dental abscesses become life-threatening infections and there is no home dentistry worth the name.
- Sleep, food and warmth do more for immune function than any supplement.
