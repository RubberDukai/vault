# A communications plan — template

A radio nobody is listening to is furniture. A frequency only one person remembers is a secret, not a channel. The plan is what turns equipment into communication, and it is a piece of paper — printed, copied, and in every bag.

Fill this in for your own household and neighbours. Print it. **Do not rely on any device to hold it.**

## The PACE principle

For every link you care about, agree **four** ways to make it, in order:

- **Primary** — what you use normally.
- **Alternate** — what you use when the primary is down.
- **Contingency** — what you use when both are down.
- **Emergency** — the last resort that always works.

The point is that everyone knows the fallback *without discussing it*, because when the primary fails there may be no way to discuss it.

An example for a household split between home and a workplace 20 km away:

| | Method | When it fails |
|---|---|---|
| **P** | Mobile phone | Network down or overloaded |
| **A** | Meshtastic, private channel | Out of range, node battery flat |
| **C** | PMR446 radio, channel 8, at the scheduled times | Beyond a few km |
| **E** | Meet at the agreed rally point | — |

## The plan

Copy this and fill in the blanks.

```
COMMUNICATIONS PLAN — [household / group name]
Version [n], dated [date]. Replaces all earlier versions.

PEOPLE
  Name        Call / short name     Carries             Usual location
  __________  ___________________   _________________   ______________
  __________  ___________________   _________________   ______________

PRIMARY      [e.g. mobile phone — numbers on the back of this sheet]
ALTERNATE    [e.g. Meshtastic — channel "____", key held by ______]
CONTINGENCY  [e.g. PMR446 ch __ / CB ch __ / amateur ___._ MHz]
EMERGENCY    [e.g. rally point: _________________ ]

SCHEDULE (when to listen, if not always)
  Every day at  ____  and  ____  (local time), for ten minutes.
  Listen first. Transmit only if you have something to say.

RALLY POINTS
  1st: ______________________  (if unreachable, go to 2nd)
  2nd: ______________________
  3rd: ______________________

CODES
  "All well"              = routine, nothing needed
  "Need help, not urgent" = come when you can
  "Emergency"             = drop everything, come now
  Duress word: ________   (means "I am not free to speak" — act accordingly)

RULES
  1. Say who you are, who you want, then the message. Short.
  2. Repeat anything important twice.
  3. If you hear nothing at the schedule, try the next method. Do not panic.
  4. Keep this sheet with you. Keep a copy at home. Do not photograph it.

Maintained by: __________   Next review: __________
```

## Notes on filling it in

**Schedules beat monitoring.** Nobody can listen to a radio all day, and a radio that is always on flattens its battery. Two fixed windows a day — say 08:00 and 20:00, ten minutes each — mean everyone knows when to be listening and when they are free not to.

**Listen first.** Most of the value of a radio is in hearing. A group that listens for ten minutes and says nothing has still confirmed the network works.

**Rally points** solve the case where nothing electronic works at all. Pick places everyone knows, can walk to, and can wait at safely. A first and a second, in case the first is the problem.

**Codes** are not secrecy, they are speed. "All well" said once carries more, faster, than a conversation. The **duress word** is the one that matters: an ordinary-sounding word that means "someone is making me say this". Agree it, never write down what it is, and never use it lightly.

**Message format** for anything that matters: **who it is for, who it is from, the time, the message, what you need and by when.** A message with no time and no sender is nearly useless an hour later.

**Keep a log.** Time, who, what. A notebook by the radio. It settles arguments, spots patterns, and is how the next person picks up where you left off.

## Radio etiquette that keeps a channel usable

- Wait a moment before transmitting — someone else may be mid-message.
- Press, pause, speak. The first half-second after keying is often cut off.
- Speak slowly, clearly, a little louder than normal, with the microphone a hand's width away.
- Spell anything ambiguous using the phonetic alphabet: Alpha, Bravo, Charlie, Delta, Echo, Foxtrot, Golf, Hotel, India, Juliet, Kilo, Lima, Mike, November, Oscar, Papa, Quebec, Romeo, Sierra, Tango, Uniform, Victor, Whiskey, X-ray, Yankee, Zulu.
- Numbers one digit at a time: "one four five", not "a hundred and forty-five".
- **Say "over"** when you have finished and expect a reply, **"out"** when the conversation is done. Never both.
- Do not chat. A shared channel is shared.

## Review it

Every six months, or whenever anyone joins or leaves or a piece of kit changes: reissue the sheet with a new version number, and collect the old ones. Two versions of a plan in circulation is worse than none.

And **practise**. One drill a season — a schedule kept, a message passed by each method, a walk to the rally point — is what makes the plan real. The first time you use a radio should not be the day you need it.
