# Making water safe to drink

The single most important idea on this page: **filtering and disinfecting are different jobs, and doing one does not do the other.**

- **Filtering** removes dirt, silt and cloudiness, and depending on the filter, bacteria and protozoa. It does not reliably remove viruses.
- **Disinfecting** kills living organisms. It does not remove chemicals, silt or bad taste, and it works far less well in cloudy water.

Clear water is not safe water. The organisms that will hurt you are invisible.

## The order of operations

1. **Settle** — let the water stand so heavy sediment drops out. An hour helps; overnight is better.
2. **Pre-filter** — pour through cloth, a sand filter or a commercial filter to remove the remaining cloudiness.
3. **Disinfect** — boil, chlorinate or use sunlight.
4. **Store safely** — in a clean, covered container with a tap or a narrow neck, so nobody dips dirty hands into it.

Skipping step 2 is the most common mistake. Chemical disinfection is dramatically less effective in cloudy water, because pathogens hide inside the suspended particles.

## Boiling — the method that never fails

Boiling kills everything biological: bacteria, viruses, protozoa, worms. It cannot be done wrong, it needs no supplies, and it does not expire.

- Bring to a **rolling boil for 1 minute**.
- Above roughly **2,000 m / 6,500 ft**, boil for **3 minutes** — water boils cooler at altitude.

That is the whole procedure. Longer boiling is fuel wasted, not extra safety. Boiling does not remove chemicals, salt or heavy metals.

The cost is fuel. Where fuel is short, use boiling for drinking water for the very young, the elderly and the sick, and use chemical treatment for everyone else.

## Chlorine — cheap, fast, storable

Use **unscented household bleach at 5–6% sodium hypochlorite**. Not scented, not colour-safe, no added detergent.

| Water | Bleach per litre | Bleach per gallon (US) |
|---|---|---|
| Clear | 2 drops | 8 drops (1/8 tsp) |
| Cloudy or very cold | 4 drops | 16 drops (1/4 tsp) |

Stir, then **wait 30 minutes**. Afterwards it should smell faintly of swimming pool. If it does not, add the same dose again and wait another 15 minutes. If it still does not, your bleach has lost its strength — discard it as a treatment supply.

> **Warning** Liquid bleach degrades. It loses roughly 20% of its strength per year at room temperature, faster if warm or in sunlight, and it is effectively useless after about a year. Rotate it. For long-term storage, **calcium hypochlorite granules** ("pool shock", 65–70%) keep for years in a sealed, dry, cool container and make far more treated water per kilogram. Handle it dry, keep it away from acids and anything flammable, and never store it in a metal container.

Chlorine does not reliably kill *Cryptosporidium*, a protozoan that causes severe diarrhoea. If you suspect it, boil or filter instead.

## Sunlight (SODIS) — free, slow, surprisingly good

For when you have no fuel and no chemicals:

1. Use a **clear plastic (PET) bottle, 2 litres or smaller**. Not glass — it blocks the useful ultraviolet.
2. Fill with **clear** water. If you can't read text through the full bottle, it is too cloudy — filter it first.
3. Lay it on its side in **direct sun for at least 6 hours**. On an overcast day, **two full days**.
4. A dark or reflective surface underneath (corrugated iron, dark stone) raises the temperature and helps.

SODIS reliably kills bacteria and viruses. It is less effective against some protozoal cysts. It costs nothing and needs no resupply, which is exactly why it belongs in your head.

## Filters, and what they actually do

| Type | Removes | Does not remove |
|---|---|---|
| Cloth, sand, coffee filter | Silt, grit, larger debris | Anything microbiological |
| Ceramic / hollow fibre (0.1–0.2 micron) | Bacteria, protozoa | Viruses, chemicals |
| Activated carbon | Taste, odour, chlorine, some chemicals | Most pathogens |
| Reverse osmosis | Almost everything including salt | Needs pressure, wastes water |
| Distillation | Everything, including salt and heavy metals | Nothing — but very slow and fuel-hungry |

In practice: **a 0.2 micron filter followed by chlorine or boiling covers every realistic threat.**

## Building a slow sand filter

A biosand filter is a barrel of graded sand that you can build from local materials and that will run for years. Water sits above the sand; a biological layer forms in the top centimetres and does most of the pathogen removal.

From bottom to top inside a barrel with an outlet pipe at the base:

1. **10 cm of coarse gravel** around the outlet pipe.
2. **5 cm of fine gravel**.
3. **50 cm of clean, washed fine sand** — this is the working layer.
4. A **diffuser plate** — a perforated tray — so pouring water in does not gouge the sand.
5. Keep **5 cm of standing water** above the sand at all times. This is what keeps the biological layer alive.

It takes **one to three weeks** for that layer to establish before the filter performs properly. It removes most bacteria and protozoa and a lot of the turbidity. It does **not** make water safe on its own — still disinfect afterwards.

## Rainwater

Rain is close to distilled when it leaves the sky and picks up whatever is on your roof on the way down. Divert and discard the **first few minutes** of any rainfall — a "first flush" diverter does this automatically — then collect. Cover the tank against light and mosquitoes. Treat before drinking if anyone is vulnerable.

Roughly: **1 mm of rain on 1 m² of roof gives 1 litre.** A 60 m² roof in 10 mm of rain yields around 600 litres, less perhaps 10% for splash and losses.

## How much do you need?

- **Survival minimum:** 3 litres per person per day, drinking only, sedentary, temperate.
- **Realistic:** 15 litres per person per day for drinking, cooking and basic washing.
- **Comfortable:** 50 litres and up.

Hot weather, hard physical work, illness, pregnancy and breastfeeding all push these numbers up sharply. Someone with diarrhoea can need several litres a day just to stay level.

## What to keep on hand

- A 0.2 micron filter, plus spare cartridges
- Calcium hypochlorite granules, sealed and dry, and a way to measure small doses
- A large pot with a lid dedicated to boiling
- Several clear PET bottles for SODIS
- Clean covered containers for storage, with taps rather than open tops
- Cloth for pre-filtering
