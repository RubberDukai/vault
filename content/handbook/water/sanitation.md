# Sanitation and waste

In every collapse, famine and refugee crisis on record, the big killer is not violence or starvation. It is diarrhoeal disease — cholera, dysentery, typhoid, hepatitis A — spread by human waste getting back into mouths via water, hands, flies and food.

This is the most preventable category of death there is, and prevention is almost entirely about **separation and handwashing**.

## The rules that matter

1. **Latrines at least 30 m from any water source, and downhill from it.**
2. **The base of the pit must sit at least 1.5 m above the water table.**
3. **Cover waste** — soil, ash, lime or sawdust after each use. Cuts smell and stops flies.
4. **A lid on the hole.** Flies are the main vector between waste and food.
5. **Handwashing at the exit of the latrine, every time.** Not near it. At it.

If you do only one thing from this page, do the handwashing.

## Handwashing without running water

A **tippy tap**: a jerrycan or bottle hung on a frame with a small hole near the cap, tipped by a foot lever on a string. Nobody touches the container, so nobody recontaminates it. Cost: nothing. Effect: the single largest reduction in diarrhoeal disease of any intervention.

Soap matters more than water volume. Soap breaks the lipid envelope of viruses and lifts bacteria off skin so they rinse away. Wash for **20 seconds**, including between fingers, thumbs and under nails.

Wash after using the latrine, after handling waste or bodies, after touching animals, before preparing food, before eating, and before treating a wound.

If you have no soap: **wood ash** is mildly alkaline and abrasive and works far better than nothing. Sand and water is next best.

## Types of latrine

**Simple pit latrine.** A hole 1–2 m deep, a slab or logs with a squat hole, a superstructure for privacy, a lid. Cheap, fast, works. Fills and must be moved.

**Ventilated improved pit (VIP).** As above, with a vertical vent pipe painted black and fly-screened at the top. The pipe heats in the sun and draws air up out of the pit, taking smell with it. Flies inside fly toward the light at the top of the pipe and die against the screen. Keep the superstructure dark inside so the pipe is the brightest exit.

**Composting / dry toilet.** Urine diverted separately, solids covered with sawdust, ash or dry leaves after each use. No water needed. Two chambers used alternately: fill one, seal it, use the other, and by the time the second is full the first has composted.

> **Warning** Composted human waste needs a long time before it can safely touch food crops — a minimum of a year, and two is better, or thermophilic composting held above 55 °C throughout. Ascaris eggs in particular survive for years. Use finished compost on trees, hedges and non-food plants, not on salad.

**Urine.** Sterile in a healthy person and a genuinely good nitrogen fertiliser. Dilute roughly **1 part urine to 10 parts water** and apply to soil, never to leaves you will eat.

## Greywater

Water from washing is not clean, but it is not sewage. Run it out onto a mulch basin or a gravel trench well away from the drinking water source. Do not let it pool — standing water breeds mosquitoes within about a week.

## Rubbish

- **Burn** what burns cleanly, upwind and away from where people sleep. Do not burn plastics near food or living space if you can avoid it.
- **Compost** all food waste, kept covered so it does not feed rats.
- **Bury** the rest, deep, away from water.
- **Keep metal, glass, wire and containers.** In a world with no shops these are raw material, not rubbish. Sort them somewhere dry.

## When people get sick

Assume anyone with diarrhoea is shedding pathogens in enormous quantities.

- Their waste goes in a dedicated, covered container, buried deep or heavily disinfected.
- Their hands, and the hands of anyone caring for them, get washed obsessively.
- They get **oral rehydration solution**, not plain water. See the medical module.
- Keep them away from food preparation entirely until 48 hours after symptoms stop.

## Bodies

This needs saying plainly because it is where a lot of bad folklore lives. **Bodies of people who died of trauma are not a major disease risk.** The urgent risk is to the people handling them, and it is mainly from the diseases the person already had. Panic-driven mass burials do more harm than good, largely because they destroy the ability to identify the dead, which matters enormously to the living afterwards.

- Handle with gloves or improvised barriers, wash thoroughly afterwards.
- Bury at least **1.5 m deep**, at least **30 m from water sources** and downhill.
- **Record who, when and where**, and mark the grave. Someone will need that later.
- The exception is death from cholera or a haemorrhagic fever, where the body is genuinely infectious and should be handled minimally by as few people as possible.
